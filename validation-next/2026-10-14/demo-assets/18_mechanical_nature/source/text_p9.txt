PIM specimens, including ultrasonic washing in absolute ethanol for
10 min and UV post-curing at 60 °C for 60 min.
Mechanical experiments
Supplementary Figs. 17–20 summarizes the test conﬁgurations used in
this study, employing a ZwickRoell Z100 universal tensile test machine.
Supplementary Table 2 details the load rates, directions, and geome-
tries for various mechanical tests satisfying quasi-static load condi-
tions. ASTM D638 standard guidelines informed the mechanical
evaluation of parent materials, with specimens conforming to ASTM
D638 type IV (Supplementary Fig. 17). PIM samples featuring pre-
programmed strengthening mechanisms and CIM designs were tested
in a 20 × 10 × 20 mm3 region along the x, y, and z axes, subject to
vertical tensile or compressive forces (Supplementary Fig. 18). Tensile
testing for CIM+PIM composite layers focused on polymer chain
loading, employing specimens with twoCIM+PIM cells aligned with the
load direction and one cell perpendicular, reducing necking phe-
nomena and enhancing the generalizability of mechanical properties
(Supplementary Fig. 19a). Compressive tests for crystal loading
mechanics used a single CIM+PIM cell perpendicular to the load
direction (Supplementary Fig. 19b). Biomimetic shells underwent
tensile and compressive testing to assess reshaping and compression
mechanics (Supplementary Fig. 19c, d). A single grabbing ﬁnger was
tested for both stretching (grab force) and bending (adapting force)
(Supplementary Fig. 19e, f). Cylindrical tissue test specimens with a
molecular-like density of 4.25 cm−3 under different PS levels were
designed to evaluate tissue mechanics (Supplementary Fig. 20a). For
robotic toe and ﬁnger joints, specialized ﬁxtures simulated bending
loads akin to human tissue dynamics (Supplementary Fig. 20b–d).

Digital image correlation analysis
The DIC analysis is performed on a Correlated Solutions VIC-3D DIC
measurement system using its 2D measuring conﬁgurations (Supple-
mentary Fig. 21). The DIC is used to measure full-ﬁeld surface strains
during mechanical testing of PIM samples. These spatial strain maps
were used to locate regions associated with different strengthening
events (entangled zones, crosslinked segments, and proto-crystalline
chains) and quantify the relationship between experimental local chain
strain and the programmed PIM design, such as robotic muscles. The
speckle patterns are sprayed evenly on the specimens for DIC calcu-
lations. A Basler acA4112-30 um camera with 28-mm Basler lens are
used to capture the relative positions of the DIC speckle patterns, while
VIC-2D 7 software is used to perform DIC analysis based on the
experimental results. The analyzed images possess a resolution of
4096 pixels × 3000 pixels. A 19 pixels × 19 pixels subset dimension is
used during the DIC derivation, while the step size is 4 pixels.
